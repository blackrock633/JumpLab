# Deterministic training rules

`src/core/training.ts` owns normalized input validation, generation and adaptation. New prescriptions and adaptation use `conservative/2`. Earlier prescriptions keep their recorded version. Identical inputs and version produce identical sessions; IDs for cloud writes are assigned outside generation. There is no LLM or remote training service.

Onboarding records jump goal, new/trained experience, available days, local start hour, starting date, weekly/session time budgets, no equipment/dumbbells/barbell, body weight and display units, optional known squat **working load**, no-impact/no-loaded-squats limitation, current pain and hard practice days. Pounds convert with 0.45359237 kg/lb. Unknown strength is valid; users never need a maximum lift. Unsupported individual limitations require qualified individual guidance rather than invented exercises. Profile changes explain future-plan replacement and retain completed records.

New users receive up to two 25-minute sessions/week with two sets per movement; trained users up to three 35-minute sessions with three sets. Weekly/session budgets cap actual scheduling. Days are deterministically sorted and selected with at least one full intervening day between hard sessions/practice, including the weekly boundary. No available recovery-safe day or insufficient time returns an explanation. Session estimates include five minutes of easy warmup, three minutes cooldown, execution and prescribed rest, with spare time for transitions; they are template estimates, not measured duration.

Replacing a profile moves past start dates forward and conservatively starts no earlier than the third calendar date after the latest completed workout, preserving recovery even if the replacement changes weekdays. It does not delete earlier prescriptions or completed work.

Existing workouts open on their prescribed local calendar day. The separate replacement-plan floor never disables a workout on that day.

Each prescription includes instructions, sets/reps, load or effort, rest, duration, date/hour and progression version. Strength work is chair/goblet/barbell squat as permitted, plus calf raises. Goal selects submaximal vertical, controlled two-step or low repeated jumps; repeated work uses five reps versus three. No-impact omits jumping, no-loaded-squats uses a chair squat. Optional known working load initializes a conservative 40% starting load; unknown loads use effort guidance, leaving at least three repetitions in reserve. Squats use six reps, calf raises eight, rest 120 seconds for strength/jumps and 60 for calf raises. Start light and stop if pain or landing control deteriorates.

Every fourth week reduces sets; generation uses one set and adaptation halves prior sets (floor, minimum one). Restoring baseline sets after a deload requires an explicitly comfortable, fully completed deload prescription and no conflicting deload feedback. Loads hold. Optional effort and recovery initially have no value. Earlier records with default feedback cannot qualify for increases. With three distinct, previously unused comfortable completed sessions (effort ≤6, good recovery, no pain), load rises by **at most 2.5%**, rounded down to 0.01 kg. Unloaded strength may gain one rep up to eight; jump contacts never automatically increase. Workout evidence IDs are retained to prevent repeated escalation. The app uses the prior 21 days of observations from the same program and requires an observation from the current plan before adapting from that window. Missing work does not count as failure. No observations holds prescriptions, except scheduled deload. Two recent efforts ≥9 reduce sets by 20% (floor, minimum one). Compatible, validated trends need at least three measurements and >5% decline to reduce volume; trends never accelerate increases. Current experimental measurements are never passed to adaptation and remain ineligible for training.

Pain or unusual fatigue sets future work to zero and displays stop/modify guidance. Resuming requires profile review; no automatic escalation occurs. Adaptation shifts the entire schedule by enough whole weeks to preserve the practice days and clear the recovery window after the latest actual completion. Unfinished workouts are available on their scheduled date. The new future plan records its reason and rule version and preserves completed logs. No missed sessions are moved or crammed into adjacent days. Prior prescriptions remain stored for history/corrections. Offline workout feedback persists immediately in SQLite and queues a stable account-scoped write.

Established exercise principles motivating the templates include progressive resistance, individualization, recovery, technique and monitoring fatigue. References to consult: ACSM, *Progression Models in Resistance Training for Healthy Adults*, Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2009;41(3):687–708, doi:10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670; NSCA, *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning*, 4th ed., 2016; and WHO *Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour*, 2020. These sources do **not** validate this exact program or detector. The 25/35-minute templates, 40% initial working load, 2.5% cap, three-observation gate, 5% trend threshold, four-week deload and spacing/practice exclusion are conservative **product heuristics** requiring coach/user evaluation.
